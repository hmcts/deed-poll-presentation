Before the meeting

Rehearse the story in AAT

Steve has confirmed KBDP2026-00003 is Received, with no documents and no existing referral. These are presenter-reported facts; upload, preview and referral actions still need rehearsal. Tick the checks only after checking the actual environment. Ticks reset when the page is reloaded.

Prepare a case story, not a race through every screen. Use one case for review and referral, then a clearly identified prepared case for a later stage. Explain the change of case; do not imply that signing, posting or returning paperwork happened during the demo.

References to have beside you: review/referral case KBDP2026-00003 · later-stage case __________ · two-page demonstration PDF

Audience emphasis. For the Senior Master, give judicial consideration and the clarity of directions the most time. For the wider stakeholder group, emphasise connected working, role responsibilities and the remaining release checks. Tomorrow’s slot length can be adjusted once confirmed.

00:00–02:00

Open with the purpose

A working staff and judicial service, with room for feedback.

Show

  1. Use the attendee overview briefly. Set the scope: AAT, example cases, staff and judicial case management.
  2. Explain that you will show a short case journey, then invite questions.

Suggested words

“Today I’d like to show you the Deed Poll service as a working system. I’ll follow the work of the court staff and the judiciary: finding a case, reviewing its evidence, referring it for consideration and recording what happens next.

I’ll keep the walkthrough to around twenty minutes so we have time for your feedback. The aim is to build confidence in what has been delivered and hear what we should refine or confirm before release.”
02:00–04:00

1. Find the work

Staff can move from the worklist to the case they need.

Show

  1. Open the staff case list. Briefly point out the status filters and work queues.
  2. Search for KBDP2026-00003 by reference; open it.
  3. If it is useful and rehearsed, show a saved query. Avoid building a complex filter live.

Suggested words

“This is the starting point for court staff. They can look across their cases, focus on a particular status or return to a saved view. This example is currently Received. I’m opening it so we can follow its details and history, then review a demonstration document and the judicial handover.”

If needed: If the list is slow, use the pre-opened case tab. Describe the navigation and continue; do not spend the demonstration investigating the environment.

04:00–07:00

2. Review the application and its evidence

The information needed to understand the case is connected.

Show

  1. Orient the audience to KBDP2026-00003, its Received status and the application details.
  2. Open the documents section and the two-page demonstration PDF attached during preparation. If it has not been attached yet, upload it once using an appropriate document type, then open it in the viewer.
  3. Move from page 1 to page 2, show zoom if useful, then point out Download original and return to the case.

Suggested words

“The application details and its evidence are available in the same case context. Here I can read a supporting document without losing my place, move between documents and still download the original.

The point is to make the information needed for a decision easier to find and understand.”

If needed: If a format cannot preview, show the original-download option and the rehearsed PDF. Say what happened plainly; do not describe unverified conversion as working.

07:00–10:00

3. Record the casework

The case shows the work done and the next action.

Show

  1. Show the witness information and a relevant casework tab.
  2. Add a short, clearly synthetic case note, if rehearsed. Save and show it in the history.
  3. Explain that the displayed case status follows the recorded business dates. Keep referral progress distinct from case status.

Suggested words

“Staff can record the checks and actions they have taken, alongside the application and its evidence. I’m adding a short demonstration note so that the next person can understand what has been done.

The case status reflects the recorded business dates. A judicial referral has its own progress, which we’ll look at next.”

If needed: If saving is unavailable, show the existing synthetic note and explain the expected action. Keep the example small; avoid changing business dates just to produce a status.

10:00–15:00

4. Hand over for judicial consideration

The judicial user has the case context and a clear route to record a direction.

Show

  1. In the staff view of KBDP2026-00003, open the Referrals tab and select Raise a referral. Choose Other and enter: Demonstration referral for KBDP2026-00003 — judicial review workflow rehearsal. Set the priority to Urgent for this AAT rehearsal. If an urgency explanation is required, enter: AAT demonstration only — prioritised to make this referral visible during the rehearsal. There was no referral at Steve’s initial check; if rehearsal has since created one, use it rather than raising a duplicate.
  2. Switch to the separate judicial session. The worklist shows the ten oldest referrals, with urgent referrals at the top; there is no search. Find KBDP2026-00003 among the urgent referrals, then claim the example and open it.
  3. Show the application, witnesses and supporting evidence available to the judicial user.
  4. Record a synthetic direction and show its check/confirmation step. Point out My referrals.
  5. Return to the staff session and show the direction available for action. Mention revision/withdrawal of the judge’s own direction before closure only if useful.

Referral reasons shown in AAT

  • Applicant is a minor
  • Domestic abuse considerations
  • Non-publication request
  • Declarant requirements not met
  • Separated or estranged
  • Other

Suggested words

“Where an application needs judicial consideration, court staff raise a referral explaining what needs to be considered. The referral stays linked to the application and its evidence.

Now we’ll switch to the judicial view and find the referral we’ve just raised. The judicial worklist shows the ten oldest referrals, with urgent referrals taking priority. We’ve marked this demonstration referral urgent so we can follow it through.

A judicial user can claim a referral and review the application and its supporting evidence.

The direction is then recorded back against that referral for the court staff to action. It is a visible handover, with the application and the decision kept together.”

If needed: Use a prepared referral if creation or claiming is unavailable. If another judge holds it, use the agreed fallback; do not take over someone else’s work. For today’s audience, invite a brief observation here and hold longer discussion until the close.

15:00–18:00

5. Action the direction and show a later stage

Staff remain responsible for progressing the application.

Show

  1. In the staff view, show the direction and how staff record that it has been actioned. Close only the intended synthetic referral, if rehearsed.
  2. Open the clearly identified later-stage example, prepared with appropriate witness and signature information.
  3. Show how the recorded dates support progress towards enrolment, and the history that explains the case.

Suggested words

“The direction is available to the court staff to action; it does not silently rewrite the application’s dates. The staff record the next business step.

I’m now moving to a prepared example at a later stage. The signing and return of paperwork take place outside this short demonstration. This lets us see how staff record that later work and follow the application towards enrolment.”

If needed: Show the prepared state rather than trying to complete an invalid sequence. Do not suggest that sending forms automatically sets the signature-requested date or that the service has posted a sealed deed.

18:00–20:00

6. Keep correspondence with the case

Staff can check the message and follow its recorded outcome.

Show

  1. If rehearsal passed, open a provisioned correspondence example, show the preview and check step, then the case’s email history.
  2. Where useful, point out generated deed forms and their link to the application.
  3. Stop before Send unless a controlled test recipient and the intended send were agreed. If unavailable, use these two minutes to recap the core journey.

Suggested words

“Correspondence is managed in the context of the application. Staff can check the wording before sending, and the record of the request and its outcome stays with the case.

For this demonstration I’m showing the preview and history. A message being submitted is not the same as proof that it has been received.”

If needed: A missing template or unavailable preview is an environment condition to record. Do not repeatedly press Send or promise that an uncertain outcome means no email went out.

20:00–22:00

Close with the feedback you need

Confidence in delivered capability, with clear input to the release.

Show

  1. Return to the attendee overview. Summarise the connected staff/judicial journey in one sentence.
  2. Be specific about any AAT limitations seen. Keep release approval separate from this demonstration.
  3. Open discussion with the three questions below.

Suggested words

“We’ve followed an application from the staff worklist through its evidence, casework and judicial consideration, then looked at how the next stage and correspondence stay with the case.

That is the capability we have built. The next step is to confirm that the workflow meets the court’s needs and complete the remaining release and operational checks.

I’d welcome your feedback: does this reflect how you need to work; is the information needed for a decision easy to find; and what should we resolve before release?”

Use selectively · not extra time by default

Optional features and likely questions

Gazette publication

For a rehearsed Delivery Manager session, show the ready list, mandatory case review and batch confirmation/history. Swap this for correspondence; do not extend the core walkthrough. Sending is disabled by default in the source configuration. Do not turn it on or send a batch for the demonstration.

Configurable referral window

The API implementation supports a Delivery Manager-controlled inactivity window. Include the settings page only if it is actually deployed and rehearsed in AAT; the documentation snapshot did not include its admin page. Otherwise mention it only in answer to a relevant question.

The online citizen journey

“The citizen application and payment journey is a separate stage of delivery. Today I’m demonstrating the staff and judicial service.” Do not present the mock-backed citizen flow as a working end-to-end submission/payment service.

“Is it ready for release?”

“The demonstration shows the capability that has been delivered. We are confirming the workflow with users and completing the release and operational acceptance checks. I’ll separate any outstanding items from the functionality you’ve seen today.” Use a dated release checklist if asked for a formal readiness decision.

Discussion · approximately 22:00 onwards

Listen for decisions, examples and priorities

  1. Does this reflect the way you need to work?
  2. Can you find the information needed to review and progress a case?
  3. What must we resolve before release?

Capture each point as an observation, a proposed improvement or a decision needed. Confirm the intended meaning and priority. If an answer is not known, note an owner and follow-up rather than making a commitment in the room.

If time is tight: keep the worklist → evidence → judicial referral → staff action → closing questions. Drop optional features first. If AAT is unavailable, use the overview to explain the workflow, state clearly that the live demonstration could not be completed, and arrange a follow-up.
